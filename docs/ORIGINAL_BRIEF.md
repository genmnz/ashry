## Key Findings

### 1. Has this already been done?

**The commercial and biological core is mature.** Precision fermentation — programming microbes via recombinant DNA to make animal proteins through controlled fermentation — is already on supermarket shelves. Perfect Day produces bovine β-lactoglobulin (brand "ProFerm") using a genetically engineered strain of the filamentous fungus *Trichoderma reesei*\[1\] (production strain QM6a-PD1), cleared by the FDA under GRAS Notice GRN 863\[2\] in March 2020. Impossible Foods produces soy leghemoglobin in *Pichia pastoris*\[3\] (now reclassified *Komagataella phaffii*),\[3\] strain MXY0291, built by inserting multiple copies of the soybean LGB2 gene plus eight extra copies of *P. pastoris* heme-biosynthesis genes; it was cleared under GRAS Notice GRN 737. These exactly match the two hosts named on your slide, so your project rests on established, citable biology.

**The literature base is rich and recent.** Several 2024–2026 reviews anchor the field: Nielsen, Meyer & Arnau, "The Next Food Revolution Is Here" (*Annual Review of Food Science and Technology*, 2024, vol. 15, 173–187);\[4\] a mini-review by Eastham & Leman in *Current Opinion in Food Science* (2024, 58:101194); and a 2025 review, "Toward Rational Design of Precision-Fermented Milk Proteins"\[5\] (PMC13500877), which lays out exactly the cross-species-selection + PTM + AI-optimization logic a computational thesis would use. A dedicated review, "Downstream Processing of Food Proteins from Precision Fermentation" (*Annual Review of Food Science and Technology*, 2026), covers the purification-cost challenge on your slide.

**Purely computational precedents exist for individual steps — but not the whole pipeline.** The subagent's targeted search found: (a) an in-silico whey-protein design study doing codon-optimized α-lactalbumin and β-lactoglobulin constructs, but in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*,\[6\] not *K. phaffii*; (b) a 2026 bioRxiv preprint computationally redesigning β-lactoglobulin\[7\] with AlphaFold3, ProteinMPNN and PyRosetta; (c) a 2025 PLOS ONE coarse-grained molecular-dynamics study of κ- and β-casein aggregates\[8\] built from AlphaFold structures and the MARTINI force field; (d) an in-silico study determining *Pichia pastoris* signal peptides with SignalP, Phobius and WolfPsort;\[9\] and (e) a 2026 GitHub flux-balance project identifying metabolic bottlenecks for recombinant proteins in *K. phaffii* (adapting the iMT1026 model),\[10\] though for pharma proteins, not food proteins. There are also wet-lab iGEM precedents — iGEM Düsseldorf 2019 expressed all four caseins plus α-lactalbumin and β-lactoglobulin,\[11\] and a 2014 DIYbio team codon-optimized caseins for yeast. **No single integrated computational thesis spanning all five food proteins in these two hosts, with a techno-economic layer, was found.** That gap is your contribution.

### 2. The scientific challenge, quantified (your slide's "core challenge")

The slide correctly identifies folding, post-translational modification (PTM) and downstream cost as the live problems. The literature lets you put numbers and mechanisms behind each:

- **A clear tractability hierarchy exists among the targets.**\[5\] β-lactoglobulin and α-lactalbumin are the most tractable because they are small, well-defined globular proteins with minimal PTM dependency; caseins and lactoferrin are hardest because they require intracellular phosphorylation and glycosylation that microbial hosts do not natively provide. This hierarchy is stated explicitly in the 2025 "Toward Rational Design" review and the 2026 *Trends in Biotechnology*-adjacent opinion "Precision-fermented milk proteins as a model for industrial food biotechnology."\[12\]
- **Casein is the signature hard problem.** Caseins are intrinsically disordered phosphoproteins whose food function (micelle formation, cheese melt/stretch, calcium carrying) depends on phosphorylation of specific serine residues that nucleate colloidal calcium phosphate nanoclusters. Microbial hosts do not phosphorylate casein natively. Wageningen work (*Food Research International*, 2023, 173:113315) showed dephosphorylated caseins lose micelle-forming ability,\[13\] while a 2025 *Trends in Biotechnology* paper engineered *Bacillus subtilis* kinases (PrkD, YabT) into *E. coli* to produce phosphorylated, functional αs1-casein. This is the richest single bottleneck for a computational deep-dive.
- **Titers are real but modest at research scale.** The peer-reviewed benchmark for β-lactoglobulin in *T. reesei* is Aro, Ercili-Cura, Andberg, Silventoinen, Lille, Hosia, Nordlund & Landowski, Food Research International 163:112131 (Jan 2023), whose highlights state: "Production of 1 g/L of beta-lactoglobulin was achieved using T. reesei production host. Production of 2 g/L of egg ovalbumin was achieved using T. reesei production host." The highest reported secretory leghemoglobin titer in P. pastoris is from Shao, Xue, Liu, Zuo, Wei, Huang, Lian & Xu, Bioresource Technology 363:127884 (Nov 2022): "the LegH titer and the heme-binding ratio reached up to 3.5 g/L and 93%, respectively, both of which represented the highest level ever reported for heme-containing proteins in GRAS microbial cell factories." Recombinant human lactoferrin has reached ~1,200 mg/L in P. pastoris per Jiang et al. 2008, Dairy Science & Technology (DOI 10.1051/dst:2007019): "A yield of approximately 1200 mg·L⁻¹ was obtained in fed-batch fermentation… improved significantly by mixed methanol/glycerol feeding at the ratio of 4:1 during the induction phase of high cell-density fermentation." These numbers give your FBA and techno-economic chapters concrete anchors.
- **Downstream purification is the cost driver.** Chromatography is designed for high-purity, high-value pharma products and is too expensive for bulk food proteins; reviews call for cheaper precipitation/membrane routes.\[14\] A demonstrated example: β-lactoglobulin from *P. pastoris* purified by polyphosphate precipitation raised protein content from 26 to 72 wt% with emulsification preserved (Hoppenreijs et al., *Food Research International*, 2024, 176:113801).\[15\] Yeast mannan/extracellular polysaccharide interference is a named purification obstacle for *K. phaffii* proteins.

### 3. Economic and sustainability context (for your introduction/discussion)

- **Sustainability is the headline selling point, with caveats.** The WSP USA LCA for Perfect Day (2021), ISO 14067-conformant and reviewed by an independent expert panel, reports (per the combined ISO-conformant report) GHG emissions "between 91.2% and 96.6% lower" than total protein in bovine milk (the standalone GHG report states 85–97%), with blue water 96–99% lower and non-renewable energy 29–60% lower. GFI's synthesis of 30+ LCAs gives similar ranges. But some peer-reviewed work (e.g., Behm et al., 2022) finds fermentation proteins can have *higher* emissions than efficient dairy systems depending on the electricity mix, and an Imperial College London analysis stresses that land-use savings (up to ~96%) are more robust than carbon savings. Present the ranges honestly, not as settled fact.
- **Cost is the make-or-break variable.** Public techno-economic estimates for precision-fermented protein span an enormous and under-informative range — GFI found four public models ranging from under $20/kg to roughly $15,000/kg. ## Caveats

- **Computational predictions are hypotheses, not proof.** AlphaFold confidence for intrinsically disordered caseins is intrinsically limited, FBA predicts capability not actual flux, and techno-economic models carry wide uncertainty (the public range spans under $20/kg to ~$15,000/kg). Frame every result as predictive and clearly state assumptions.
- **Some of the most useful sources are preprints, company LCAs or trade press** (bioRxiv, Perfect Day's own LCA, market-research pages, GFI). Prefer the peer-reviewed anchors (Annual Reviews, *Food Research International*, *Bioresource Technology*, *Trends in Biotechnology*) for load-bearing claims and label the rest as industry/preliminary.

- **Tool access and compute limits are real.** AlphaFold3 and some MD runs need GPU time; plan around free Colab quotas, keep simulations short, and document hardware so results are reproducible on a student machine.
- **This report is a research-and-design assessment, not a guarantee of results.** The actual simulation and modeling outputs will depend on your parameter choices; the value of the thesis lies in doing the integration carefully and transparently, exactly as your constraints prioritize.

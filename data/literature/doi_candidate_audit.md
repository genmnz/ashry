### Hand-checked, deliberately left without a DOI (3)

The verdict above reads "no record" for these, which is the automated finding and not the whole story. Each was searched by hand:

- **entry 173**: The recorded title, "Revisiting the codon adaptation index from a theoretical perspective", does not exist in either registry. The recorded venue and year, Nucleic Acids Research 2003, match Jansen et al., `10.1093/nar/gkg306`, exactly, whose title is "Revisiting the codon adaptation index from a *whole-genome* perspective". That is most likely the intended work with the title misremembered, but the two differ at the discriminating word, so adopting it would be a guess. A human should confirm which paper the entry meant to cite.
- **entry 198**: A hand search finds Pais, Mayer and Felderer, bioRxiv 2023, `10.1101/2023.11.10.566515`, whose title opens identically but continues "...novel classes of *small proteins* using publicly available sequence-based predictors" where the entry records "...novel classes of *biologics*". Venue, year, type and the first eight words all agree, so this is probably the same preprint paraphrased, but the divergence is at a substantive noun and not a truncation, so the prefix rule correctly refuses it.
- **entry 227**: This is a book, not an article: *Milk Proteins: From Expression to Food*, edited by Thompson, Boland and Singh for Academic Press. Crossref returns only a 2009 review *of* the book, `10.1002/ajhb.20972`, which is a different work. Many edited volumes of that period carry no DOI at the book level, so no identifier is the correct outcome and the entry should cite the book by its editors and ISBN.
